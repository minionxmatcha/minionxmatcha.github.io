When the black binding thread on your bamboo matcha whisk (*chasen*) breaks or unravels, you don't need to throw the whisk away. You can easily restring it at home using embroidery floss and customize it with a charm.

Here is the complete two-part walkthrough using felt paper to explain the mechanics clearly, followed by the **zero-knot technique** to secure a charm.

---

## Part 1: How to Restring a Chasen

### Tools Needed:
* **New or loose Chasen**
* **Tweezers**
* **Utility knife & Scissors**
* **Embroidery floss** (cut to ~3 to 4 times the height of your chasen)
* **Small paper strip** (folded into a sleeve)

### Step-by-Step Restringing:
1. **Remove Old Binding:** Make one careful cut through the old thread using a utility knife. Use tweezers to pull and trim remaining bits cleanly without scoring the bamboo.
2. **Prepare the Floss:** Standard embroidery floss has 6 strands, which is too thick. Separate it and use **2 strands**.
3. **Insert the Paper Sleeve:** Fold a small piece of paper into a collar/sleeve and slide it down the center. This holds the inner prongs safely back and separates them from the outer tines.
4. **The Weave Pattern:**
   * Loop the thread around the first outer prong.
   * Bring the left thread behind the next prong while keeping the right thread pinned in place.
   * Repeat this alternating movement around the circumference.
5. **Adjust Tension:** Use tweezers to slide the thread row down so it sits straight and parallel to the bamboo node.
6. **Repeat Rows:** Repeat the loop for a second, third, or fourth row depending on your preference.

## Part 2: Secure a Charm (Minion's Zero-Knot Technique)

Adding a metal charm or pendant normally leaves a bulky knot that unravels during whisking. This zero-knot method locks the charm into the weave seamlessly.

### Key Technique Tips:
* **Choose the Right Charm:** Look for bracelet charms or jewelry pendants that have an open jump ring or top loop.
* **Keep the Last Prong Untouched:** Leave the final prong of each row naked.
* **Thread the Loop:** Separate the two dangling threads and pass all of the left thread through the charm loop.
* **Orientation Check:** Ensure the charm is right-side up before pulling the thread taut.
* **Lock It In:** Continue repeating the weave steps from Part 1 (bottom row first, then working upward). The tension of the woven rows locks the charm ring flush against the bamboo without tying any knots.
* **Thread Trimming:** Trim the finished ends slightly longer than the bamboo node to allow natural movement without slipping.
