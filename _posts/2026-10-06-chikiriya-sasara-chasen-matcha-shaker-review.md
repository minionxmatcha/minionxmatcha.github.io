Traditional matcha preparation requires a bamboo whisk (*chasen*) and a bowl (*chawan*). Kyoto tea merchant **Chikiriya (京都有機茶 治木屋)** developed the **Sasara Matcha Shaker**, dubbed the world's first "chasen shaker." Built with an internal set of resin whisk tines modeled after traditional bamboo whisk tips, it is engineered to produce lump-free micro-foam in 20 seconds.

Here is the head-to-head showdown testing bubble fineness, foam retention, and clumping against a regular shaker.

### How to Use the Sasara Shaker

1. **Water first (crucial)** Fill room-temperature or cold water up to the measured fill line. Never put dry powder into an empty shaker first, or it will stick to the corners.
2. **Add 2g matcha** Sift or spoon 2 grams of matcha powder directly on top of the water.
3. **Seal securely** Twist the lid firmly until tight.
4. **Shake for 20 seconds** Shake vigorously with a vertical motion.

---

### The Showdown: Sasara vs. Standard Shaker

* **Micro-Bubble Consistency:** Standard shaker bottles rely solely on liquid momentum, often creating large, uneven dish-soap bubbles. The internal *sasara* tines act as mechanical shear blades, breaking the water surface into a dense, velvety micro-foam.
* **The Base Check (Clumping):** Regular shakers frequently trap unmixed matcha sludge in the bottom seams. The Sasara's curved base and tine clearance allow liquid to cycle through without leaving dried powder behind.
* **Ice Gap Fill:** When poured over ice cubes, the Sasara foam suspends evenly throughout the drink, filling the crevices between the ice rather than separating quickly into clear liquid and froth.
